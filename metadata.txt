Summary: NOAA’s Coastal Ocean ReAnalysis (CORA) couples long-term water level observations with hydrodynamic modeling to create historical information between tide stations. CORA water levels are simulated with the ocean circulation model ADvanced CIRCulation (ADCIRC) coupled with the phase-averaging Simulating WAves Nearshore (SWAN) model to produce surface gravity wave spectra and account for time-averaged wave contributions. Coastal water level observations from the NOAA’s National Water Level Observation Network (NWLON) are low-pass filtered and assimilated into the model to account for long-term sea level variability and reduce model errors. As of September, 2026, domains include the U.S. East and Gulf coasts (CORA-GEC) and the U.S. West coast, Hawaii, and southern Alaska (CORA-PAC).  The time period of the reanalysis is 1979-2022 (inclusive) for the GEC and 1979-2025 (inclusive) for the PAC, with each year modeled independently.
